## 4. Thesis: 19,500 against my 18,600

The trade loses money if December settles above about $19,340 (entry $19,468 minus the $125/day cost).

### What changed this week
Spot did exactly what I did not want: seven up days and +$1,000 since the call. But the curve barely followed. December rose $32 while spot rose $1,000, so the gap between December and spot closed from about $1,400 to about $500. The market already priced this bounce last week.

### Why I still think December is too high
1. The rally is Atlantic only, and it is already fading in the Pacific. Fearnleys (11 Nov): scarce prompt ships in the North Atlantic pushed transatlantic (+$591) and fronthaul (+$378) rates up, while by midweek more open ships in South China met a thinner Australian cargo list and the Pacific round voyage eased (-$136). Atlantic squeezes fix themselves: ships ballast across.
2. Period rates fell while spot rose. One-year Kamsarmax is $20,100 (-$400 on the week) and one-year Panamax $17,500 (-$1,000) (Fearnleys). People paying for a year of a ship are not buying the rally.
3. Asian coal is still soft. China imported 39.4 Mt of coal in October, 6% below last year and below September's 43.4 Mt, as buyers refused to restock at high prices (customs data via Reuters).
4. Fearnleys' own indicators say the rebound may stall: volume growth still trails fleet growth.

### What I need
Spot to stop rising this month. If November finishes around $18,400 and December comes in close to November, my number holds. A December average of 19,500 needs spot to climb another $500 and stay there for a month.

### What keeps me small
Capesize is strong (1-year +$1,801, spot earnings +$2,208 on 11 Nov to $39,125), and that can push cargo splits onto Panamaxes. ECSA held firm for late November and early December arrivals, which goes against my point about too many ships heading to Brazil. And I have no ballaster count this week.

### What would change my mind
- Dec-26 FFA at or above $20,468 (entry plus $1,000). I close the next working day
- ECSA ballasters below about 280, or clear new December cargo demand from Brazil
- Spot above $19,500 with the Pacific firming as well, not only the Atlantic

### In plain words
The ships got more expensive this week, but only in the Atlantic, and the people who hire ships for a whole year are paying less, not more. I think this is a short squeeze, not a new trend. I am less sure than last week, so I hold what I have and add nothing.
