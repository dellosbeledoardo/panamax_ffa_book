4. Thesis: 19,000 against my 18,300

The call fails if December settles above about 18,925 (break-even after costs at a 19,050 fill).

What the curve is pricing
December at about 19,000, 4% above November. In five years, December beat November by that much once: 2023, when the Panama Canal cut transits and December ran 17% above November. In 2024 and 2025 December fell 12 to 13%. The curve is treating 2026 as a disruption year.

Why I think it is too high
The ships are already on their way. Global Panamax ballasters went from about 780 in early October to about 850, and about 310 are heading for East Coast South America. A ballast leg from the Indian Ocean takes 25 to 30 days, so they arrive for December loading, when Brazilian soybeans are out of season and corn is mostly shipped.
Asian coal is the core cargo, and it is soft. Indonesian exports are down year on year for a fourth month, Chinese import prices are falling, and Australian coal is moving back to Capesizes in November.
Paper moved before spot, and moved evenly. December rose $1,228 from 30 Oct to 4 Nov while spot was flat, with every month up by a similar amount on the day Capesize jumped 7%. That reads as mood and spillover more than new Panamax information.

What I need
November has to finish near 17,900, so spot averaging about 18,100 for the rest of the month instead of the roughly 18,500 the curve implies. Then a December 2% above November, not 4%.

What keeps me small
The disruption is real, just a different one. European gas is above 100 EUR/MWh and coal imports there rose 79% in October, Capesize strength is pushing cargo splits onto Panamaxes, and Panamax earns less than a smaller Supramax. Three up days in spot say the market disagrees with me today. Size is 10 days.

What would change my mind
December above my entry plus $1,000. I close the next working day.
ECSA ballasters below about 280 in the Signal weekly monitor, or a clear pickup in December cargo from Brazil.

In plain words
Too many empty ships are sailing to Brazil for the month when Brazil has the least to load. The market is pricing a busy December anyway. I think it will be busier than usual, but not that busy.
